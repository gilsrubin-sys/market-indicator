    Note: Market Indicator is a replication of Helene Meisler's breadth overbought/oversold method (her published thresholds for the NYSE and Nasdaq oscillators, Nasdaq Momentum, 30-day A-D, Summation "net needed", Volume Indicator and Hi-Lo Indicator) mapped onto a 0–100 scale. It is not a number she publishes. Short-term = 10-day NYSE Oscillator + Nasdaq Momentum; Medium-term = 30-day A-D, net-needed, Volume Indicator, Hi-Lo. Rows marked "context" are shown for reference and are not in the score. Zones: 0–20 Extreme Oversold · 20–40 Oversold · 40–60 Neutral · 60–80 Overbought · 80–100 Extreme Overbought. Each component's score is anchored so that her own oversold level = 40 (the edge of the Oversold zone) and her overbought level = 60; "Extreme" is reserved for readings beyond what she called merely oversold. Weights: NYSE Oscillator 40%, Nasdaq Momentum 10%, 30-day A-D 14.3%, net-needed 14.3%, Volume Indicator 7.1% (formula unconfirmed, bias-adjusted), Hi-Lo NYSE 7.1%, Hi-Lo Nasdaq 7.1%. Good oversold (her rule) = short-term AND medium-term oversold AND bearish sentiment (10-day total put/call ≥ .97, "top of the page"; or .92–.97 confirmed by the NAAIM Exposure Index in fear, ≤ 65). NAAIM feeds only this flag, never the score; a bare "Oversold" reading without it is not her buy signal. Recommended exposure is a model output about broad US-market exposure (gross, for a book with beta ≈ 1), derived from this breadth/sentiment state: 100% when overbought or neutral (don't chase when overbought), 80% invested / 20% cash when oversold but not a good oversold, 1.25x on a good oversold (100% if only the NAAIM tiebreak fires), with hold rules and leverage guards. It is not personal advice and not a portfolio risk model; the backtest covers only Feb 2024–Sep 2026 with 3 good-oversold episodes and did not beat a static 100% on Sharpe. Updated Sep 25, 2026 after an independent review. Not investment advice.
